Interview feedback reference guide Keep this open alongside your Ashby form · One section per Ashby section Section 1 / 3 Interviewer's decision Score (1–4) The score should follow from the assessment — not the other way around. Don't decide the score first and then look for justifications. Good Score 4 grounded in 2 concrete behavioral examples with measurable outcomes. Avoid Score 4 with an empty recap. Or score 2 with "not senior enough" and no supporting example. Short recap — Strengths & Warnings 2–4 sentences max. This is the first thing the hiring manager and recruiter read. It must make sense to someone who wasn't in the room. Good "Strong ownership. Stepped into a project stalled for 6 weeks, delivered in 3. Reduced delays 30% via a new dashboard. Watch out: tends to execute before aligning stakeholders." Avoid "Great candidate, seems to fit the role. Maybe not senior enough. A bit of bullshitting at some point." Every strength = a fact. Every warning = an observed behavior. No impressions, no gut feelings, no comparisons to other candidates. Section 2 / 3 Assessment Hard skills Role-specific technical skills. Write what you observed — not what the candidate claimed to be good at. Good "Strong operational problem-solving — uses data to isolate inefficiencies. E.g. analyzed tracking logs to find a bottleneck → workflow redesign → 30% fewer delays over 4 weeks." Avoid "Relevant sales experience. Technical skills seem solid for the role." At least 1 concrete example: context + action + result (with a number if possible). Communication skills StructureSynthesizingStraightforwardListening What you observed during the interview — not a general impression of the person. Good "Consistently answers in context → action → result format. Rephrased an ambiguous question before answering. Clear synthesis at the end of longer responses." Avoid "Good communication." / "Speaks well." / "Has a strong accent when speaking French." Legal risk — never write this Accent, native language, or manner of speaking Language level used as a discriminating criterion "Not very clear" without an example of what was unclear Infinite drive OwnershipEntrepreneurialExtra mileUrgencyHigh standards Look for a situation where the candidate took the lead without being asked, in a difficult or ambiguous context. Good "An internal tool had been stalled for 6 weeks with no clear owner. The candidate stepped in, clarified responsibilities, set up weekly check-ins with product and engineering → delivered in 3 weeks." Avoid "Seems proactive." / "Appears very motivated for the role." / "Gives the impression of being involved." Expected structure: Situation → What they did (unprompted) → Result. If you don't have a concrete example, leave the field blank and flag it as a follow-up point. Unblocking questions: "Tell me about a time you took ownership of something that wasn't formally yours." / "What's a decision you made entirely on your own and are proud of?" Invent & simplify PrecisionVelocityAmbitionResults Did they simplify a process, automate something, remove unnecessary steps? Give the before and after. Good "The team was manually compiling reports from 5 tools — 5 hrs/week. The candidate built a centralized dashboard → down to under 1 hr/week, with real-time visibility." Avoid "Mentioned having improved reporting processes." / "Seems capable of simplifying things." Ideal format: Before [situation] → Action [what they did] → After [concrete measure]. Unblocking questions: "Describe a process you simplified. What concretely changed?" / "How do you handle a request to deliver faster without dropping quality?" Section 3 / 3 Candidate questions What they asked Note the actual questions asked. They reveal the candidate's priorities and what might block a decision. Good "How does Vizzia measure operational impact? How is project ownership structured across teams?" Avoid "Asked about the role and the team." / "Seemed curious and engaged." Copy questions word for word if possible — useful context for the next interviewer. Checklist before submitting Every dimension is backed by at least 1 example with context + action + result No mention of accent, age, appearance, family situation, or nationality Warnings are grounded in observed behavior, not impressions The recap makes sense to someone who wasn't in the room Candidate questions are copied as-is, not paraphrased
